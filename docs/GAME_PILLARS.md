# Underworld — Game Pillars

## 1. Core identity — LOCKED

Underworld is a survival/exploration game with a comparatively readable surface world above a substantially larger, deeper and more mysterious underground world.

The surface is not a lobby. It supports long-term settlement, exploration, resources, combat and world familiarity. The Underworld is the primary long-term exploration space and the main source of spatial mystery.

## 2. The hidden world exists independently — LOCKED

Important underground content is generated from the world seed independently of the player's future choices and progression.

A rare boss, structure, cavern, resource region or cave junction may exist beneath a place where the player later spends hundreds of hours building. This is allowed to happen by coincidence; the game must not move important content simply because a player built above it.

Progression changes what the player can realistically survive and exploit. It does not decide what is allowed to exist.

## 3. Geography should remain meaningful — LOCKED

Old regions should not become irrelevant simply because the player progressed past their original enemies.

Surface and underground geography can gain new meaning later through newly discovered connections, deeper routes, hidden structures, rare resources, lairs and exits.

The game should prefer spatial relevance over universal enemy level scaling.

## 4. Player freedom with consequences — LOCKED

Do not forbid reasonable player solutions unless a hard technical or encounter requirement demands it.

Examples:

- underground bases are allowed wherever normal building rules and geometry permit;
- a player may repurpose old boss locations when the encounter design allows it;
- the environment, limited space, hostile creatures and logistics should make difficult choices difficult naturally;
- structural/critical geometry may be protected where necessary.

## 5. Procedural first, intelligently connected — LOCKED

The world should remain procedural and unpredictable. It should not become a hand-authored Souls map.

However, the underground generator should deliberately create a minority of high-value spatial connections and loops. The design shorthand is **about 10% Souls-style connectivity**: enough for occasional "wait, this connects back THERE" moments, not enough to turn the world into a tightly authored shortcut puzzle.

## 6. Depth changes generation grammar — LOCKED

Shallow, mid and deep Underworld are not identical caves with different textures and enemy stats.

Depth changes topology, geometry, connectivity, ecology and gameplay tendencies. Local exceptions are allowed so the layers remain unpredictable.

## 7. Combat direction — DIRECTIONAL

Combat should be more active and readable than baseline survival-game combat, but it should not drift into an MMO ability-bar system.

Universal actions and weapon properties should carry most of the depth. Enemy behavior, telegraphs, spacing and timing matter more than simply increasing stats.

Exact future defensive mechanics, weapons and moves remain open until they are needed.

## 8. Mining should be intrinsically satisfying — LOCKED

Repeated resource gathering must feel good mechanically because underground resource extraction will be a major long-term activity.

Small nodes and large deposits intentionally provide different experiences; see `MINING_AND_RESOURCES.md`.

## 9. Do not design by feature accumulation — LOCKED

New features require a reason tied to the pillars above or to a current development milestone.

A feature being interesting is not enough reason to add it immediately.
